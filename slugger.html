Lyman Moore & Lyseth · 2002–2005

You had a plan. Why are you changing it?

The material below is sourced from the Portland Press Herald archive. All the information is publicly available and verifiable. Articles involving specific children are not linked, but are available in the Press Herald archives. No information is taken from the Maine CDC cancer cluster investigation records described on the Investigation page. Each citation links to the original scanned notice or article.

18 months after “Portland gets a final plan for schools” had declared a task force had produced a “final proposal” to renovate Portland's “aging elementary schools” for $76.8 million, no elementary school renovations have begun. The proposed plan had new elementary schools being completed in 2006, and renovations completed in 2007.

2002

6/8/2002 - “City has 10-year plan for schools - The proposal closes two of 10 elementary schools and rehabilitates the rest, but the cost is unknown.”

“The first phase of the proposal - which has construction starting in 2004 - calls for building a new Jack Elementary School on Munjoy Hill. The new Jack would open in 2006, when nearby Adams School would close and send its students to Jack.”

“Down the line, Presumpscot School also would close and its students would go to a new and bigger Baxter School, which would open in 2008. That would leave eight mainland elementary schools, plus schools on Cliff and Peaks islands, which also would be renovated.”

“The plan has 10 phases, and the targeted completion date to renovate or rebuild all the schools is 2014. School board Chairwoman Michelle Hedrich stressed that even if the proposal is adopted, it could change over time. 'One think I want to avoid is citizens saying, 'You had a plan. Why are you changing it?' she said. 'We don't know what the next decade is going to bring us.'”

“Still, the proposal provides a starting point to help realize a years-long goal of the city to upgrade its aging elementary schools, said Superintendent Mary Jo O'Connor. 'It's basically the first stab at moving forward,' she said.”

“The plan, presented to the school board this week, stems from the recent news that Portland is likely to get some state aid to rebuild or renovate Jack School, which closed last August because of toxic mold.”

Lyseth renovation is noted as “Phase 4” although there is no timeline provided for when “Phase 4” would begin.

Portland Press Herald · 6/8/2002

6/13/2002 - “Experts studying mold at ski area - No health problems have been reported, says Sunday River, which has contracted with a Westbrook firm to inspect one of its hotels.”

“Engineers are expected to conduct tests in the hotel over the next few weeks to determine the extent of the problem. Mold is becoming more widely recognized as a health concern in buildings across the United States. It typically grows in damp environments, often caused by roof or plumbing leaks.”

“There are many types of mold, however, and most pose no trouble for healthy people. But toxic mold has shut down buildings in Maine, including Jack School in Portland and Lincolnville Central School. And because there are no clear federal regulations on exposure limits or cleanup methods, dealing with mold can be complex.”

“The Sunday River mold was found last month when workers were starting renovations on the hotel's steam room and discovered mold growing behind a wall, Duplessis said. They recognized the potential for trouble and notified management.”

“Sunday River has contracted with Biosafe Environmental Services, of Westbrook to inspect the building and recommend remedial action. Mark Coleman, the company's president, said Wednesday that it's too early to characterize the problem or clearly identify the type of mold that's present.”

“A team of structural and mechanical engineers will soon inspect the building, he said. 'There isn't enough data to offer any conclusion or interpretation,' he said.”

Portland Press Herald · 6/13/2002

2003

4/15/2003 - “Slow start comes to quick end” is an article about the Portland Sea Dogs baseball team, but ends with talking about the 'Strike Out Cancer in Kids' program.

“Pitcher Greg Montalbano won Sunday's game but was apologizing to 7-year-old (child) of Portland. 'Only one strikeout,' Montalano said. 'I'll have to do better.' Montalbano knows the importance of strikeouts in the Sea Dogs' Strike Out Cancer in Kids program. Money is pledged for every strikeout recorded by a Sea Dogs pitcher to benefit the Maine Children's Cancer Program.”

“(Name), a first-grader at Lyseth School, was introduced as this year's 'Slugger's Kid' for the program. He was diagnosed with a form of leukemia 19 months ago. He is in remission but still undergoes treatments.”

4/26/2003 - “One little boy, a huge symbol of hope” another article about the Lyseth first grader being treated for leukemia.

“It may become routine to hear Hadlock Field announcer Dean Rogers say 'that's another Strike Out Cancer in Kids strike out...' but God forbid this program ever get taken for granted. This is the Sea Dogs' version of the Boston Red Sox's well-publicized Jimmy Fund, which raises money for the Dana-Farber Cancer Institute in Boston.”

“It's a reminder of priorities, that being emotional about baseball is still a distant second to being passionate about life. Especially, the life of a child.”

“The hope increased a month later when, after intense chemotherapy treatments, (Child) cancer was in remission. He would still require weekly chemo treatments for two years, but the signs were positive. To administer the treatments, (Name) has a tube in his chest. At his mom's request, he readily lifts his shirt to show the tube. He smiles when he does it. Come to think of it, he smiles a lot.”

“When the smile is not there, the determination behind it remains. Every Tuesday, (Child) receives his chemo, either at home, or at the cancer center. It makes him sick and tired and, on Wednesday mornings, he does not look so good. Every Wednesday morning, (Parent) makes a suggestion that (Child) stay home from school and, every Wednesday morning, (Child) declines the day off and heads to Lyseth School, where he is a first-grader in (Teacher) class.”

“Last spring, the (Parents) considered having (Child) not play baseball. He looked frail and was tired often. 'We had to get the OK from the doctors,' (Parent) said. 'If the doctors said no, I'd be comfortable with that. Part of me wished they would say no. But they said let him do it. He missed a few games because of fatigue and whatnot. And he looked pale. Some parents must have thought we were crazy. But it was a very positive experience. He loved every minute of it.'”

“It was the glove that Weatherby wore when he pitched his first professional game. It was big for (Child)'s hand, but it didn't stop him from immediately putting it on, beaming with wide eyes. It was a keepsake, but Weatherby saw a more useful purpose for it. 'That's something he can have,' Weatherby said, 'and when he gets older, he'll have a good glove.'”

“It's a nice glove, but more encouraging is the thought of that glove someday fitting on the hand of (Child). When he gets older.”

10/X/2003 - another Lyseth Kindergartener is diagnosed with leukemia. The second in two years, and sixth child to have cancer at Lyseth and Lyman Moore in the past 4 years.

10/5/2003 - “Great gift for kids to help a great program”

“The 'book of great things to do' was featured at a publication party held at the Maine Cancer Center in Scarborough. Sales profits will benefit the Maine Children's Cancer Program.”

“Chris Rogers, MCCP board president, introduced Susan Whitehouse, chairwoman of the book project, and Dr. Craig Hurwitz, program medical director. (Parents) and their children also represented the program. (Child), 8, was a patient at the center.”

“Project contributors included some well-known Mainers, several of whom were present. U.S. Sen. Susan Collins described how she gathers sea glass and friendship stones in Madawaska Lake. Gov. John Baldacci called Maine-ly Fun a wonderful gift to the children of the state. His suggestion for free time fun was biking at Acadia National Park. Dave Santoro, WGME chief meteorologist, recommended building weather instruments.”

2004

4/20/2004 - The Lyseth student diagnosed with leukemia in 2001, which had been in remission, has metastasized.

5/16/2004 - “Slugger's Kid thrown a curve”

“(Child) underwent blood tests that same day and got preliminary results back on April 20. That is when his dad, sat down with (Child). It was not a chat about school, or how to make contact with a level swing. (Dad) looked his son in the eyes and told him, 'We don't know for sure, but we may still have a fight on our hands.'”

“(Child) looked back with those dark brown eyes of his, the ones that always communicate hope and enthusiasm. They filled with tears.”

“When (Child) served as the Slugger's Kid, his leukemia was in remission. He still underwent chemotherapy treatments, but the going looked good. Those treatments ended last October, about the time when (Child)'s term as Slugger's Kid ended.”

“But in April, (Child) complained that his ankle was sore. Then his shoulder ached. 'Joint pain was a real red flag,' (Mom) said. So, on April 17, while (Child) was at Hadlock, watching the Sea Dogs with friends, (Mom) was on the phone, describing the symptoms.”

“(Child) planned to return to Hadlock on May 1 for the annual Strike Out Cancer In Kids day. Instead (Child) watched from his room at Maine Medical Center, while listening to the game on the radio.”

“After a nine-day stay in the hospital, (Child) was discharged. But the real battle is only beginning. While undergoing chemotherapy, (Child) awaits a bone marrow transplant. The search is ongoing for a donor who matches up with (Child).”

“And (Child) is not the only Lyseth student in need. (Child 2), 5, a kindergarten student, also has a form of leukemia, diagnosed last October. He is undergoing chemo and may eventually need a bone marrow transplant.”

“Juanita McLeod, the Lyseth school nurse, is on a committee head by Lyman Moore Middle School Principal Steve Rogers, that came together to help the two boys. On June 5 (the same day as the Lyseth spring fair), a bone marrow drive will be held. The effort is not only an attempt to find a match for the boys, but to also build up the National Bone Marrow Registry, which is trying to aid 3,000 patients nationwide.”

“Meanwhile, (Child) waits. When he received chemo treatments last year, he found the energy to go to school. He loves school. But now, 'he's just too tired,' (Mom) said. So he stays home. 'It's more important to keep him as healthy as possible'.”

“The (Parents) did allow (Child) a final moment of baseball joy before he restarted his treatments. On April 24, he was able to play in the last game of the indoor baseball season. 'And he really ripped one,' (Dad) said, describing (Child)'s blast to center field.”

“The Slugger's Kid is really a slugger. But before he can play baseball again, (Child) has a fight on his hands.”

2005

4/24/2005 - “Legal Advertisement, Portland Public Schools, Notice to Bidders - Asbestos Removal Services”

“Sealed bids will be received at the Portland Public Schools Business Office, attention: Mr. Randy Stewart, Director of Business Services...until Thursday May 12”

“Summer 2005 Asbestos Abatement - Lyseth Elementary School Phase II. A site investigation and mandatory pre-bid meeting will be held at Presumpscot School...Thursday April 28”

“Additional Information related to this project can be obtained from the Facilities Engineer, Mr. Douglas Sherwood at (phone) or the Project Designer, Mr. Mark P. Coleman, Environmental Safety & Hygiene Associates, Inc. at (phone).”

Portland Press Herald · 4/24/2005

The Notice runs in the paper on Sunday, April 24th, and has a mandatory pre-bid meeting four days later on Thursday the 28th. Less than a week's notice, just as the Presumpscot abatements in 2003 and 2004. This notice specifies “Phase II”, but there is no asbestos abatement at Lyseth prior to this project on record with Maine DEP.

7/5/2005 - Records obtained directly from the Maine Department of Environmental Protection state asbestos abatement was completed by Biosafe Environmental Services. Mark Coleman is listed as the President of Biosafe Environmental Services in the 6/13/2002 Press Herald article about mold at Sunday River.

Mark Coleman is also listed as the Project Designer on the Notice to Bidders for asbestos removal at Presumpscot, and Lyseth, under “Environmental Safety & Hygiene Associates, Inc.”

The records from the Maine DEP state: 100 Ln. Feet of pipe, and 5400 Sq. Feet of ceiling tiles, were removed as a “Renovation” at “Lyseth School, 175 Auburn St, RM 1-6 & Hall” between 7/5/2005 and 7/22/2005.

9/23/2005 - the 4th child in 5.5 years passed away from cancer while attending Lyseth or Lyman Moore. At least two additional children were diagnosed with cancer at Lyseth during this timespan.

Between 5/1/1999 and 10/2/2005 there are at least 19 articles in the Portland Press Herald documenting children having cancer at Lyseth and Lyman Moore. In the 7 year span of October 1998 to October 2005, there are only two months where a child at Lyseth or Lyman Moore was not undergoing treatment for cancer.

Sourced from the Portland Press Herald archive (newspapers.com). These records are independent of, and were not obtained through, the Maine Freedom of Access Act request described on the Records page.
