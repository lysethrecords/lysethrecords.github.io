North Deering Neighborhood Cancer Data

Data provided by Maine CDC (report Tables 1–29), organized by age group. Rates are compared to North Deering.

Correction, September 29, 2026: the (0-19) population figures were incorrect.

The 0-19 population figures in the state's March 2009 draft tables (11,760 for North Deering, 55,500 for the rest of Portland) are in error and count only males. The 0-19 female population was omitted, while the case counts include both sexes. This site reproduced the state's figures, resulting in data errors. The Ages 0-19 tab, the Comparison view and the Analysis page have now been recalculated.

What changed:

  • North Deering's childhood all-cancer rate is 40.1 per 100,000 per year, not 85. The rest of Portland is 17.7, not 34.2 (Maine: 18.2).
  • Expected childhood cases at Maine's rate are 4.5, not 2.1. North Deering's 10 observed cases are about 2.2x the expected number (was 4.8x). The p-value remains significant at 0.036, but much less so than the 0.0001 previously calculated. Against Cumberland County it is 0.069 (was 0.0004), just outside of statistical significance. Against the rest of Portland it remains significant at 0.030 (was 0.0169).
  • Myeloid leukemia (p = 0.054 vs Maine, was 0.0133) and Hodgkin lymphoma (p = 0.12, was 0.0318) are no longer significant against any comparison.
  • Comparison view: North Deering child population increased from 1,176 to 2,491. This changes the figures to "1 in 249" from "1 in 118".
  • The combined result on the Analysis page changed substantially, from about 1 in 13,389 to about 1 in 90.

The ages 20-44 and older tables are unchanged. The uncorrected pages are kept for the record: Data page as first published · Analysis page as first published · Original state files.

How to read the data: For each cancer site North Deering's rate and case count are shown at the top of the table. Rate per 100k is a standardized measurement to compare different size populations. "Expected Count" is how many cases North Deering would have if cancer occurred at the same rate as the comparison population. The p-value is how often a count at least this far from the expected count would turn up if North Deering’s true rate matched the comparison population’s. It is not the probability that chance explains the result. Below 0.05 is the conventional cut-off for “statistically significant,” echoed in the Result column. Population is the actual population multiplied by 10 (years studied); in other words, each person is counted each year.
A note on the probability values: The original data file had an error in its probability column: =POISSON(observed, expected, FALSE). The FALSE returns the probability of exactly the observed count, not the cumulative tail a significance test requires. Changing the "FALSE" to "TRUE" in the formula fixes the error. The p-values here are recalculated as a two-sided Poisson test. Original data available in the Reports & Data section.
