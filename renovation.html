Lyman Moore & Lyseth · 1988–1999

Determined by Politics

The material below is sourced from the Portland Press Herald archive. All the information is publicly available and verifiable. No information is taken from the Maine CDC cancer cluster investigation records described on the Investigation page. Each citation links to the original scanned notice or article.

While investigating a request made by Lyseth Elementary to the Maine CDC to assess the rate of cancer in the Lyseth school community, I found the following articles. The articles describe the process of renovating three Portland middle schools, including Lyman Moore.

Lyseth Elementary School and Lyman Moore Middle School share a campus located at 175 Auburn Street in Portland, Maine. Lyman Moore was constructed in 1954 followed by Lyseth in 1958. An addition was completed at Lyman Moore in 1959 and at Lyseth in 1965.

Both schools were heated via a steam heat plant located in Lyman Moore. Steam heat is generated in a boiler and pushed through underground cast iron pipes to Lyseth. The steam is distributed through Lyseth in cast iron pipes with return lines for condensation using gravity to take water back to the boiler in Lyman Moore. These pipes were commonly insulated with asbestos wrapped around them.

According to a Legal Notice posted in the Portland Press Herald dated 1/4/1996, all Portland Public Schools were inspected for asbestos in 1988 in compliance with AHERA, and reinspected in 1991/1992. [PPH, 1/4/1996]

1988

In April of 1988 the Portland School Committee approved a 10 year, $37 million capital improvement plan. Joyce Rogers is noted as one of two members who voted against the plan.

“Rogers, who has just been elected secretary-treasurer of the 100,000 member National School Boards Association (she will become its president in 1991), said she was worried about the timing of asbestos removal from the walls and ceilings of some of the schools in the plan.”

“Varied amendments were proposed during an hour and a half of board debate, most aimed at changing the timing of projects so that asbestos removal coincided with the renovation of schools. In the end, all the amendments failed or were withdrawn. Committee member Richard J. McDonough repeatedly argued that the amendments would spend too much money during the early years of the plan.”

Portland Press Herald · 4/7/1988

The article also notes $1.7 million to be spent on asbestos removal. It further specifies $1.25 million of the total is for “removal and replacement of asbestos-laden ceilings at the Longfellow School.”

An article published 9/2/1988 in the Evening Express states Portland Schools spent $500,000 over the summer removing asbestos from the following locations: Portland High, Deering High, three island schools, Clifford Elementary, Presumpscot Elementary, Lyman Moore Middle, Central Office, the maintenance shop, and West School.

Evening Express · 9/2/1988

1990

A 1990 report by the Middle School Renovations Task Force stated $18.9 million would be needed to renovate Lyman Moore and King Middle School.

“In that plan, the crumbling Lincoln Middle School built in 1897 and expanded in 1913, would be replaced.”

The report suggested City Manager Robert Ganley “re-examine” the $15 million budget for the middle school renovation, specifically noting “asbestos throughout” Lyman Moore and “a serious asbestos problem” at King.

Portland Press Herald · 7/24/1995

1993

In 1993 a $14.8m bond was passed to renovate three Portland middle schools, including Lyman Moore. Lyman Moore Principal Charles Pressey leaves his position as principal to take a role overseeing the middle school renovation project. This is documented in the PPH article on 6/19/1993 and states Pressey begins the new role July 1st. [PPH, 6/19/1993]

Less than two weeks later on 6/30/1993 a picture in the PPH titled “Fire at Lyman Moore” depicts fire fighters in protective gear outside Lyman Moore and on the roof. The picture is captioned:

“Portland firefighters check out the Lyman Moore Middle School Tuesday afternoon after extinguishing a small fire on the roof. The fire appeared to have been set, according to Deputy Chief Reginald Wyman. Eight of the firefighters were removed from the scene when they were exposed to asbestos as they tore up the roof to determine the extent of the fire. Their equipment will undergo tests and the firefighters themselves also may be tested, depending on how much asbestos the gear picked up, Wyman said.”

Portland Press Herald · 6/30/1993

An article titled “School renovations may be more costly than city expected” is published 12/27/1993. It states initial estimates for renovations are $2m higher than expected, placing the cost at $17m.

“The higher cost estimate is the result of architects' suggestions in a number of cases to replace things such as roofs, windows, and boilers, which is more costly than patching and repairing what already exists, Ganley and Pressey said.”

City Manager Robert Ganley is paraphrased: “Ganley said the architects involved had been told they will need to hold the line. That could mean painful reckoning among those who want the best for their children.”

Portland Press Herald · 12/27/1993

1994

On 2/9/1994 the committee in charge of middle school renovations voted unanimously to not ask voters to approve an additional $3m for the project, and keep costs under the $14.8m previously approved. [PPH, 2/9/1994]

A 9/21/1994 article states the bids for the middle school renovations came in $1,147,819 under budget, leaving room for “amenities.” [PPH, 9/21/1994]

1995

“Unexpected costs strain school project” is printed in the PPH 6/14/1995.

“And more than $900,000 in unanticipated costs have accumulated for removing cancer-causing asbestos from the Lincoln, King, and Lyman Moore middle schools. Officials are vowing to keep the cost within the $14.8 million bond that voters approved two years ago.”

“The project has threatened to exceed its budget since it began. In early 1994, the first cost estimates came in $2 million higher than the $14.8 million voters approved.”

“'Remember February a year ago,' said Portland Design Team's David Webster. 'Nothing has changed since then. It's just that the asbestos has added to our woes and misery.' Asbestos regulations that took effect last summer have made the work more costly and complicated at all three schools.”

Portland Press Herald · 6/14/1995

A month later on 7/20/1995, the cost overruns for asbestos removal are stated as $550,000 — $450,000 less than the $900,000 figure stated the month prior.

“But renovation crews have uncovered structural problems at the 98-year-old Lincoln Middle School, and an additional $550,000 in unanticipated costs were racked up this spring for removing cancer-causing asbestos from the Lincoln, King and Lyman Moore middle schools.”

“Charles Pressey, who is overseeing the middle school renovation project, said $550,000 of the current cost overrun stems from changes in deferral asbestos regulations made last summer. He said school officials did not know those changes were coming when the $14.8 million project was budgeted and approved by voters in 1993. Those regulations require more highly trained workers to handle asbestos removal — workers who cost a lot more than originally budgeted, Pressey said.”

Portland Press Herald · 7/20/1995

7/24/1995: “School woes present tough choice — Unexpected building problems at the city's three middle schools could trigger a political fight.”

“The test dramatically showed that raw sewage from the nearly century-old building was regularly seeping into storm lines that empty into Casco Bay. That sewage is supposed to flow into Portland's waste treatment plant. Lincoln's faulty sewage lines were added to the list of problems uncovered during the $14.8 million renovation and expansion of Portland's three middle schools. It is a list that grows daily.”

“The mounting problems are pushing cost overruns past the $1 million mark, prompting school officials to quietly talk about seeking more money from voters this November in a bond issue. It will be an uphill fight. City Manager Robert Ganley said in an interview that he is against asking voters to approve more debt.”

“Long before the first shovels hit the dirt in the fall of 1994, there were clear signs that $14.8 million might not cover the cost of renovating the three schools. Yet political and economic pressures ended up shaping the project's budget as much, if not more, than engineering reports about potential trouble spots.”

“Back in 1989, Ganley set up a 10-year spending plan for all construction and renovation projects. He budgeted $15 million for improvements to Portland's three crowded and rundown middle schools.”

“At the same time, a Middle School Renovations Task Force was finishing a report that said $18.9 million would be needed…The final report from the task force, released in June 1990, recommended that the city 're-examine' the $15 million that Ganley had budgeted for the three schools.”

“That report noted trouble spots at all three locations. It found a 'serious asbestos problem' at King Middle School and asbestos 'throughout' Lyman Moore. And as for Lincoln, the report listed concerns about leaky and inadequate roofs and a variety of fire safety violations.”

“Soon after that report, another group, called the Middle School Facilities Committee, was formed to study the issue again. That committee hired the Portland architectural/engineering firm of Stevens Morton Rose & Thompson Inc. But from the beginning SMRT knew that whatever it recommended, it would have to fall within Ganley's $15 million limit.”

“'The figure of $15 million (in our report) was to a great extent determined by the politics of the city's capital spending plan,' said Paul Stevens, SMRT's president. 'The question to us wasn't how much should the city spend, but could we make it work with that budget.'”

“Though SMRT told the city the plan would work for $14.8 million, it also qualified its findings. It said the firm did not study asbestos in the three buildings and what it would cost to remove it.”

Portland Press Herald · 7/24/1995

In November of 1995 Portland voters approved an additional $1.2 million dollar bond to finish the middle school renovations. Students continued to attend the three schools while renovations occurred, and the project was completed in summer of 1996.

1996

On 9/30/1996 the City of Portland School Department posts two Notice To Bidders in the PPH. The first states “Boiler Retubing Lyman Moore Middle School,” with bids accepted until 10/16/1996, when they would be publicly opened and read. The second is for “Asbestos containing material abatement — Central Administration Building,” with bids opened and read 10/15/1996. [PPH, 9/30/1996]

1999

On 6/12/1999 a “Notice To Contractors, Short Form, Public School Project” was posted by Portland Public Schools for the “Lyman Moore Middle School Boiler Replacement Project.” Bids were scheduled to be opened 6/23/1999. The notice states:

“The Project includes, but is not necessarily limited to, the following: Provide a fully operational boiler plant which meets all applicable codes. Prepare boiler room for asbestos abatement, independent of this contract, by de-activating boiler system electrical service and oil feed system and removal of salvageable components. Remove the existing boilers, breaching brick base, feed water tank, feed water pumps, oil handlings system, electrical conduit and wiring serving the boilers, feed water system, oil handling system, and boiler controls. Provide temporary access by removal and replacement of boiler room wall, lawn, and sidewalk. Clean existing fuel oil storage tank. Install (2) 150 HP low-pressure steam boilers with all required components and systems.”

“Proposals must be accompanied by a certified or cashiers check for 5% of the Proposal, or a satisfactory Bid Bond in a similar amount. The Owner reserves the right to waive all formalities, and reject any and all Proposals, or to accept any proposals, or to accept any Proposal. Proposals shall be submitted on the attached proposal form. The successful Bidder will be required to furnish a 100% Contract Performance Bond and a 100% Contract Payment Bond to cover the execution of the Work which shall be in conformity with the Form of Bonds contained in Section 2-C of the specifications and for the Contract Amount. Any proposal that contains and escalation clause will be invalid.”

“A mandatory Pre-Bid Meeting will be held at the Lyman Moore Middle School 171 Auburn Street on Wednesday, June 16, 1999 at 2:00 to review the project.”

Portland Press Herald · 6/12/1999

Between April 1999 and August 2000, at least four children attending Lyseth and Lyman Moore had cancer. Three of those children passed away in that timespan.
