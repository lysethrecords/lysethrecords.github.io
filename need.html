Portland · 2007–2015

Most in Need

The material below is sourced from the Portland Press Herald archive, City of Portland budget and Capital Improvement Plan records, and records obtained directly from the Maine Department of Environmental Protection. All the information is publicly available and verifiable. No information is taken from the Maine CDC cancer cluster investigation records described on the Investigation page. Each citation links to the original scanned notice, article, or source document where available.

Seven years after “Portland gets a final plan for schools”, no comprehensive renovations on Portland's “aging elementary schools”, that are now seven years older, have begun. Jack School was abruptly closed weeks before the school year in 2001 due to toxic mold. The State funded construction of a new school. Lyseth, built in 1958 and approaching its 50th year in service, remains unrenovated.

“Asbestos Abatement at Lyseth School (Phase III). A site investigation and pre-bid meeting will be held at Lyseth School...Tuesday June 5”

“Additional Information related to this project can be obtained from the Facilities Engineer, Mr. Douglas Sherwood at (phone) or the Project Designer, Mr. Mark P. Coleman, Environmental Safety & Hygiene Associates, Inc. at (phone).”

Portland Press Herald · 5/27/2007

6/3/2007 - The same Public Notice is reposted.

Portland Press Herald · 6/3/2007

6/25/2007 - Records obtained directly from the Maine Department of Environmental Protection state asbestos abatement was completed by RJ Enterprises. “R.J. Enterprises, Inc. PO Box 82 Brunswick, ME 04011” is listed as a licensed asbestos abatement contractor on the Maine DEP site.

Records from the Maine DEP state: 20 Ln. Feet of pipe, 9850 Sq. Feet of ceiling tiles, and 9750 Sq. Feet of floor tiles were removed as a “Renovation” at “Lyseth School, 175 Auburn St, Classrm wing” between 6/25/2007 and 7/24/2007.

2008

2/19/2008 - “City school officials to ponder elementary-level redistricting - Most Portland elementary schools need repair or replacement, they say.”

“The change is fueled, in part, by a two-year controversy that preceded recent decisions to close rather than renovate the aging Clifford Elementary School and replace it with a new school elsewhere.”

“In the coming months, the school committee is expected to seriously consider redistricting, and the possibility of closing another elementary school, as it develops a first-ever comprehensive plan for the school department.”

“Four of Portland's eight remaining mainland elementary schools - Presumpscot, Longfellow, Hall and Reiche - have been identified for possible closure in one study or another since 1994.”

“The new school will be built with $18 million in state aid. Clifford will close when the new school opens in September 2011.”

“Fixing Reiche would cost $19 million; Longfellow, $18 million; Hall $30 million; and Lyseth, $35 million, according to a September 2007 assessment by WBRC Architect-Engineers of Bangor, the firm working on the Clifford replacement project.”

“Several other issues probably will come up during the review of elementary-level facilities. Hope for additional state construction aid in the near future is slight, so Portland taxpayers probably would have to foot the bill in the short term.”

“Some people also want to close the former West Elementary School on Douglass Street, which houses a special-education program. The building has sunk, and part of it had to be closed and ventilated because it contained methane gas.”

“The committee plans to seek funding in the 2008-09 budget for a consultant to help develop the comprehensive plan.”

“In the coming months, school officials will gather and review all of the long-range planning documents produced by the district in recent years. Among them are at least four elementary-level facilities reports, including a 2006 study that proposed 10 consolidated options.”

Portland Press Herald · 2/19/2008

4/19/2008 - “Portland Schools Make A Plan...Hit A Wall”

“If Portland voters pass a referendum in June to build a new $19.7 million school on Ocean Avenue, the next elementary school to close in the city would be Presumpscot Elementary School. That, at least, is what it says in the comprehensive plan for elementary schools that the Portland School Committee adopted in 2002 after eight years of study.”

“According to the plan, Lyseth Elementary School would then be replaced or renovated, and Longfellow Elementary School would be renovated. But don't bet on any of those things happening as planned.”

“High school seniors who will march in graduation ceremonies this year were toddling into kindergarten when the School Committee began studying its elementary school structure in 1994. Since then, elementary schools have been studied by at least three task forces, one ad hoc committee and several architects, engineers and other outside experts.”

“In June, voters will decide whether to accept $19.2 million in state funds to replace Clifford with a new school, to be built on Ocean Avenue, at the site of the old Baxter School.”

“The referendum was temporarily derailed April 7, when councilors David Marshall, Kevin Donoghue and Mayor Ed Suslovic voted to block a referendum vote on accepting the money. That left only six councilors in favor of a proposed bond issue that, by ordinance, requires seven votes....The trio later agreed to reconsider their positions at the council meeting scheduled for April 28.”

“The 2002 plan calls for Hall, Lyseth and Longfellow schools to be renovated or replaced and for Presumpscot to be closed. But it's unclear what steps would be taken and when. The schools have no chance of qualifying for state funding, and it's questionable whether the city can afford the work, which has been estimated at $83 million.”

Portland Press Herald · 4/19/2008

2009

3/13/2009 - “Hope builds for schools - Federal money is on its way to Maine districts, and while the exact amounts and conditions aren't yet certain, officials are lining up improvement projects.”

“Leaders of Maine's largest school district are wasting no time anticipating how they might spend federal stimulus money to modernize, renovate or repair their buildings. They've identified at least $3.7 million worth of pressing 'shovel-ready' projects that they'd like to do when Portland gets its share of $135 million State Fiscal Stabilization money earmarked for public schools.”

“Interim Superintendent Jeanne Whynot-Vickers had Facilities Manager Doug Sherwood draw up a stimulus wish list in January. The district has about $45 million worth of pending maintenance and renovation projects and typically spends about $1 million a year on them.”

“Officials also would like to restore the exterior masonry at Portland High School ($700,000), remove asbestos from Lyseth Elementary School ($110,000), and upgrade the heating system at Portland Arts and Technology High School ($100,000), among other projects.”

Portland Press Herald · 3/13/2009

2010

7/1/2010 - A City of Portland Capital Improvement (CIP) budget provided in a Q & A Packet at a 11/17/2016 Ad Hoc Committee meeting states the following:

$150,000 for asbestos/mold abatement and $250,000 for HVAC/Boiler repair, were unfunded. $750,000 for masonry and stair repair at PHS, was funded through City CIP funding.

There is no record of schools receiving any CIP funding in 2011 and 2012 in the budget provided in the Q & A packet.

2012

11/14/2012 - “Plan would spend $46 million on city schools - A proposal to renovate four elementary schools and replace a fifth may go to voters in 2013.”

“School officials have proposed a sweeping, $46 million renovation plan for five deteriorating elementary schools in the city. The plan comes more than two decades after the last major taxpayer-funded renovation effort, and caps four years of study and effort to determine how best to improve the deteriorating schools.”

“Funding for the project is planned to go before voters in November 2013. The city has already approved $3 million this year for capital improvements, but school officials said Tuesday that the annual allotments are not enough for such a large project.”

“Renovations are planned at the Lyseth, Reiche, Longfellow and Presumpscot elementary schools. Identified for replacement is Hall Elementary School, where an electrical fire in September forced a temporary closure.”

“The five problem schools, built in the period from 1952 to 1972, present a bottleneck for the district's potential to accommodate more elementary students, a fact laid bare by the predawn fire at Hall school on Sept. 17.”

“After the blaze, administrators scrambled to place students temporarily while workers repaired the charred roof and other damage caused by roughly 7,000 gallons of water that the sprinkler system released during the fire.”

“'It's not just this particular school,' said City Councilor Nick Mavodones, speaking in the fluorescent-lit Hall Elementary School gymnasium, which doubles as a cafeteria for the school's 437 students. 'The Finance Committee (is) strongly in favor of moving forward on this.'”

“Lyseth Elementary, built in 1960, may be the most in need of an update, according to preliminary assessments. High enrollment and outdated modular classroom design have pushed students and teachers into vestibules and closets for instruction time.”

“Some building materials contain asbestos, and the school has only one large common space,...”

“The district has hired an architectural firm, Oak Point Associates of Biddeford, to evaluate the five schools....The city has budgeted $700,000 for initial planning of the improvements.”

Portland Press Herald · 11/14/2012

2013

7/1/2013 - $2.99 million in city CIP funding for schools, $300,000 for elementary evaluation, $2,000,000 “technology improvements”, no asbestos removal funding.

2014

7/1/2014 - $2.35 million in city CIP funding for schools, $1,700,000 “technology improvements”, no asbestos removal funding.

2015

2/5/2015 - “Parents weigh in on Hall School replacement plan”

“About 30 people attended a community meeting Wednesday to discuss the $20 million replacement of Hall Elementary School in Portland, which has qualified for state construction funds.”

“The one-story school...was built in 1956, and the building has regular problems with drainage and rotting siding. In 2012, an electrical fire at the school displaced the 400-plus students for two weeks.”

Portland Press Herald · 2/5/2015

7/1/2015 - $1.52 million in city CIP funding for schools, no asbestos removal funding.

7/15/2015 - Records obtained directly from the Maine Department of Environmental Protection state asbestos abatement was completed by Abatement Professionals Corp.

Records state: 150 Sq. Feet of floor tiles received “infrared heat, chemical wash of floor”. This work is titled as “Courtesy” at “175 Auburn Road [sic] (Lyseth School)”, between 7:00am and 3:30pm on 7/15/2015.

This is the last asbestos abatement at Lyseth on record with the Maine DEP.

There is no record of asbestos abatement to match the $110,000 earmarked from federal funds mentioned in the 3/13/2009 Press Herald article.
