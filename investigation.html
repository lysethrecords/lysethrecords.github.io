North Deering · Portland, Maine

An Unfinished Investigation

Quotes below are from email communications pertaining to the North Deering cancer cluster investigation. The source documents are available unaltered on the Records page. Any statements can be verified against the original documents.

On December 18th 2006 the Maine CDC received a letter from the Lyseth Elementary School Principal and School Nurse asking the CDC to investigate the rates of cancer in the school community. The letter was sent after a 4th child died in the past 6 years from the school community. In total the letter mentions 29 cases of cancer in the school community over the past 10 years.

Maine Cancer Registry Epidemiologist Castine Verrill emails the principal and school nurse, with CDC Director Dr. Dora Mills copied on the email on 12/26/2006.

"Dr. Dora Mills forwarded your letter of concern about cancer in the neighborhood of North Deering. The Maine Cancer Registry takes such concerns seriously and has an established protocol for evaluating these concerns. The first step is to gather information on the 29 cancer cases that have been identified. For each case, we will need to know the following:

  1. Name
  2. Gender
  3. Type of cancer (breast, lung, leukemia, etc.)
  4. Year the cancer was diagnosed
  5. The person's age when the cancer was diagnosed
  6. Where the person was living at the time of diagnosis
  7. How long the person had lived at this residence
  8. Whether the person is still alive
  9. Hospital where the person was diagnosed or treated (if known)

Once this information is received, we will attempt to verify the cases in our database. We will also search for additional cases diagnosed in residents of North Deering. If there are specific environmental concerns, please notify us of these as well. This information will then be reviewed by our Cancer Inquiry Team to determine the appropriate next steps."

Between December 26th and February 12th Lyseth officials send a letter home to parents asking permission to share the 9 points of health data requested by the CDC.

Director Mills is contacted by WGME and WMTW on 2/12/2007 after a parent reached out to the media about the letter sent home by the school requesting permission to share the 9 points of information the CDC requested in December.

"With both, I also emphasized that we geta [sic] half dozen to a dozen such inquiries every year and that over the past 25-30 years we've been doing this, we've encountered one possible cluster. However, we take all such inquiries seriously, and it takes usually several months to complete our work in answering people's questions (I purposely did not use the word 'investigation')."

"I think we thought they knew of 29 cases and we just needed to know whom they are. However, I thought our overall approach is to look at the prevalence/incidence for the geographical area concerned, and then compare that to an expected rate. Is there someone who can call the school principal, now that this is out in the news, and work with them on this? I'm concerned that the process described by the tv stations (asking for cancer cases to be reported to them) will result in a lot of unnecessary fear."

The following day 2/13/2007, Epidemiologist Verrill sends an email responding to Director Mills.

"The principal was concerned about sending us the requested information on people with cancer and violating their privacy. For this reason, a letter was sent out to the parents of all the children at the Lyseth School to request permission to share the 9 pieces of information we requested."

"The principal will send us all the information she receives, including additional cases beyond the 29, since the concern is about the North Deering neighborhood rather than the Lyseth School. We will then evaluate the information according to our routine procedure. We will look at the overall number of cases in North Deering to see if it is higher than expected."

There is no record of the 29 cases the school was aware of being evaluated by the CDC.

Maine Cancer Registry Epidemiologist Verrill leaves the Maine CDC in April 2008. Before leaving Verrill is in contact with Chris Paulu of the Environmental and Occupational Health Program. Paulu has been working hard on geocoding cases to provide to Verrill before leaving.

"It looks to me like there are 3,267 out of 3,398 mapped (96%), for the Portland-assigned addresses. That's pretty good. The remaining 4% are mostly PO Boxes or "Unknowns" with very few having an inscrutable address."

On May 22nd 2008 Alfred May of the Environmental and Occupational Health Program states Kathy Decker, also of the EOHP, would be providing contract support to the Maine Cancer Registry to finish the cancer cluster investigation.

"Cancer cluster North Deering Report—Kathy is now providing contract support for MCR to complete the analysis and report for this project; Chris will provide consult to Kathy on the data that he prepared;"

October 3rd 2008 Maine Cancer Registry Director Molly Schwenn requests a meeting with Environmental and Occupational Health Program Director Andy Smith, Chris Paulu, and Kathy Decker to discuss "North Deering neighborhood (cancer elevation in young people)". A meeting takes place on October 7th 2008.

MCR Director Molly Schwenn requests another meeting with Paulu and Decker on 10/20/2008, scheduled for 10/27/2008.

"I would like to invite or request your presence at a meeting to discuss preliminary findings on cancer cases in the North Deering neighborhood of Portland. Kathy Decker has done a lot of work for MCR, picking up the work that Castine started. It will be very helpful to me and to Kathy if you can help us evaluate the findings thus far, and provide some guidance on what more should be done. School personnel have recontacted me for information."

On March 15th 2009, a Sunday afternoon, Kathy Decker sends the draft report to MCR Director Molly Schwenn. Schwenn responds the following day saying "I haven't reviewed all of the data yet but am having trouble understanding some of the Expected numbers, or how you arrived at them." Schwenn schedules a meeting for 3/31/2009 to go over the report.

MCR Director Schwenn sends the following email to a concerned citizen on August 29th 2012.

"I will be happy to speak with you about the findings of the North Deering neighborhood cancer evaluation. I have just located the statistical work done 2008 - 2009 and would like to review it before we talk. No additional work has been done since 2009. I will also review data produced by the first epidemiologist who worked with the North Deering data. She no longer works at the Cancer Registry nor in Maine, since 2008, so she is not available."
