Portland & Biddeford · 2005–2006

Poses No Threat

The material below is sourced from the Portland Press Herald archive. All the information is publicly available and verifiable. Articles involving specific children are not linked, but are available in the Press Herald archives. No information is taken from the Maine CDC cancer cluster investigation records described on the Investigation page. Each citation links to the original scanned notice or article.

The research will now focus on the school buildings, renovations, and the officials involved. The City of Portland had not funded a major school renovation since the middle school renovations in 1993-1996. Lyseth was last renovated in 1965 and despite multiple studies and task forces there have been no comprehensive renovations started on the city's "aging elementary schools" in 2005.

2006

3/21/2006 - “City defends repairs to Marine Terminal - Officials take issue with a Boston Globe article on mold that a consultant says used outdated information.”

“A consultant hired by Portland officials has determined that the city's $1.3 million effort to repair the International Marine Terminal has addressed all concerns about toxic mold.”

“'The repairs and restoration have made this building relatively watertight, and there haven't been any problems since then,' said Mark Coleman, president of Environmental Safety and Hygiene in Westbrook. Coleman is critical of a National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health report that says the city has removed much of the visible mold in the building but has never fixed the underlying water-leakage problems.”

“The NIOSH report is based on an inspection that was done a year ago, when the city was in the middle of fixing the building Coleman said. He said the agency never returned to re-examine the building. 'It's not a true representation of the big picture,' Coleman said of the report.”

“The Boston Globe is looking into assertions of Portland officials, said Andrew Caffrey, a business editor at the newspaper.”

“NIOSH gave the city a copy of the 26-page preliminary report in October. A final version containing additional technical information is expected to be released within a few weeks, a spokesman for the agency said Monday.”

“The preliminary report, which is based on a March 2005 inspection, said extensive water damage and mold found in the terminal is the type that can lead to respiratory illness. Elevated levels of toxic mold caused Scotia Prince Cruises to evacuate the building in August 2004. The ferry company, which canceled the 2005 season, is now suing the city for $160 million in damages, according to (City Manager Joe) Gray. The city and the ferry company are in arbitration.”

“Coleman's company examined the terminal in May, September and December. In December, the firm found some mold associated with a soiled carpet. The carpet has since been removed.”

Portland Press Herald · 3/21/2006

9/1/2006 - “Mold delays Biddeford school's year”

“Biddeford - More than 600 students learned that they will get an extra six days of summer vacation Thursday when officials announced that the opening of Biddeford Primary School will be delayed because of mold.”

“The delay is meant to give school officials time to re-test the school for mold spores after isolated patches were discovered earlier this week, according to Biddeford School Superintendent Sarah-Jane Poli.”

“Mold first appeared in the primary school in mid-July when a cleaning company washed the carpeting and shut the building over a hot weekend before drying it out, Poli said. School officials hired ESHA Biosafe of Westbrook to carry out abatement procedures in early August.”

“Results of testing done last Friday became available earlier this week. The data revealed unacceptable levels of mold in certain parts of the building, Poli said. The school district has hired another company, Northeast Test Consultants of Westbrook, to carry out an independent test of the entire building. The school will remain closed until this testing is complete.”

Portland Press Herald · 9/1/2006

9/8/2006 - “Biddeford still seeking site for classes”

“After a delay caused by mold, primary students will start school Monday, but no one knows where.”

“Recent test results indicated improvement throughout most of the building, but they also revealed a continued high level of mold in one set of classrooms, said Biddeford Primary Principal Joan Warren. This means more testing and cleaning are required before students can return to the school. Warren said she did not know how long that would take.”

“'School remains closed until we're satisfied and confident that our air quality is healthy,' she said.”

“Days before the scheduled start of school Sept. 5, Biosafe did tests showing the abatement efforts had not been successful. School officials then hired another Westbrook company, Northeast Test Consultants, to carry out an independent test of the entire building.”

“At an emergency meeting of the Biddeford School Committee on Wednesday night, school officials questioned why the most recent tests showed much lower levels of mold contamination throughout most of the school, even though no abatement procedures had been carried out since the last round of testing.”

“'If the testing was accurate when readings were quite high, then why all of a sudden are they so low?' asked Biddeford Mayor Wallace Nutting, who attended the meeting. Nutting said school officials hope to settle the discrepancy with another round of testing, but results will not be available until next week.”

Portland Press Herald · 9/8/2006

9/13/2006 - “Biddeford school to remain closed for a few more weeks”

“Biddeford Primary School will remain closed for a few weeks, the superintendent said Tuesday, despite what she described as encouraging news about the extent of the building's mold problem.”

“'It still doesn't tell us why it was so high,' she said. 'They do not have an answer for that.'”

“There are still hurdles ahead. The Primary School's early childhood room needs to be cleaned of mold, and the building's heating, ventilation and air-conditioning systems also must be cleaned, Poli said at Tuesday's meeting.”

“But the superintendent said the latest test results confirm that the two-month-old mold problem is not as severe as officials feared. 'So, in a way, the report is very good,' she said.”

Portland Press Herald · 9/13/2006

9/21/2006 - “Mold at PHS not seen as a threat”

“Mold discovered in the sub-basement of Portland High School led officials to ban students and staff members from two little-used rooms Wednesday afternoon.”

“Superintendent Mary Jo O'Connor said an indoor environmental testing expert, Mark Coleman of Westbrook, confirmed on Wednesday that mold was in two former athletic rooms in an area below the basement.”

“O'Connor said the rooms have no windows or ventilation shafts connected to the rest of the school, so Coleman and a medical adviser told school officials that the mold presents no health risk to the students or staff. As a result, no evacuation was necessary, no students were displaced and the school...will remain open through the cleanup.”

“'The mold is contained in those two areas and our experts have advised us that it poses no threat,' O'Connor said.”

“The latest mold report follows several incidents that have proved costly for communities in recent years. The affected buildings have included schools in Portland, South Portland, West Bath, Rockport, Saco, Kennebunk and, most recently, Biddeford.”

“O'Connor said cheerleaders used one of the rooms at Portland High for about an hour Tuesday afternoon to make a poster. School officials contacted the cheerleaders and their parents on Wednesday and they reported no health complaints she said.”

“O'Connor said the medical adviser told her that health problems would be caused only by prolonged, sustained exposure to the mold. She said mold related health complaints will be addressed as they arise.”

“O'Connor said water infiltrated the two rooms during May's rainy weather. She said moisture also caused mold growth in a music room, which has been cleaned and subsequently tested negative for mold.”

“Mold found at the Jack Elementary School on Munjoy Hill in 2001 led to the closure of that school and construction of the $12 million East End Community School in its place. The new school opened last month.”

“An unusually humid summer in 2003 contributed to mold outbreaks in several schools around Maine and prompted state officials to warn against shampooing school rugs during the summer.”

“Still, carpet cleaning this summer caused a mold bloom at Biddeford Primary School. Removal of the mold, which is ongoing, delayed the start of school and forced the temporary relocation of 630 students. In addition, mold was discovered in Portland's International Marine Terminal in 2004. The city paid $1.3 million to address the problem and faces a $164.5 million lawsuit for lost revenue by Scotia Prince Cruises, which occupied the waterfront terminal.”

Portland Press Herald · 9/21/2006

Despite publishing multiple articles about Biddeford Primary School and the mold at the Portland International Marine Terminal, the Press Herald omits Mark Coleman's involvement in either in this article.

10/31/2006 - “Cleaner: School has mold because those at top ignored report”

“The owner of a company that tried to clean up mold at Biddeford Primary School last summer says the outbreak never would have occurred if school officials had followed his recommendations.”

“Mark Coleman, owner of Biosafe in Westbrook, has been a consultant to the Biddeford School Department for 10 years. In May, he wrote a report for Biddeford Primary School in which he advised school officials to use special cleaning practices to prevent the growth of mold in the carpets. He also recommended removing certain sections of carpeting as soon as feasible.”

“Coleman said the mold would not have appeared if school officials had followed his advice to use high-temperature steam cleaners, which kill mold, and drying methods to quickly remove all water from carpets.”

“'They basically did what we told them not to do,' he said on Monday.”

“Superintendent Sarah Jane Poli declined to comment. She referred questions to the schools' attorney, Keith Jacques of the firm Smith, Elliot, Smith and Garmey in Saco. Jacques did not return calls on Monday.”

“School officials hired Biosafe to clean up the school in August but cut ties when a test showed continued high levels of mold in late August, a week before the start of school.”

“Coleman said the school department owes him $48,000 for his work and has made no effort to pay. Coleman contends the department ignored his recommendations even after the mold appeared. When the superintendent contacted him about the mold in July, Coleman said, he came up with a plan to thoroughly clean the building and rid it of unhealthy levels of mold. He said the cleanup failed because school officials asked him to alter the plan to save money.”

“In the initial plan Coleman said, he proposed 'full containment' methods, which require cleaning walls and then covering them with plastic sheeting for protection while the rest of the room is under abatement. He said he also advised removing everything from the rooms that were being cleaned.”

“Coleman said school officials opted instead to clean around the classroom items and to put plastic on windows but not entire walls. He said he was still willing to accept the job because he thought it could be done, though it might not be as effective as what he proposed.”

“Coleman said he approached the job in two phases. For the first half of the job, he followed the school officials' methods. The second half included areas where mold growth was more pronounced. For those places, he said, he insisted on sticking to his initial recommendations.”

“When test results came back in late August, they showed that mold levels in some areas had risen since July. Coleman said he felt vindicated because five of six failed rooms were from the first phase. The sixth contaminated area, the library, was not included in either phase.”

“Coleman said it is not unusual for tests to show that further cleaning is necessary. He said that he could have finished the job for $20,000, and that the school department is now doing a cleaning that is out of proportion with the problem.”

Portland Press Herald · 10/31/2006
